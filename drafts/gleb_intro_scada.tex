\section{Introduction}

Pasteurization is a thermolabile process that determines the safety and shelf
life of dairy products. The pasteurization unit at JSC ``Savushkin Product''
operates under stringent temperature and time constraints, where even minor
deviations can compromise product quality or trigger batch rejection
\cite{ivaniuk2013neuro}. Conventional Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition
(SCADA) systems supervise this process by polling sensors, logging telemetry,
and displaying alarms to operators. However, the control logic inside a
programmable logic controller (PLC) and the tuning parameters of PID loops often
remain opaque to plant personnel. Each level resembles a black box: inputs and
outputs are visible, while the internal algorithm is hidden.

A neural network (NN),
for instance, can be represented as such a black box
(Fig.~\ref{fig:black_nn}), yet the underlying decision rules are crucial for
safe operation.

This paper builds upon the ideas discussed in
\cite{ivaniuk2025neurosymbolic,ivaniuk2024neurosemantic,Lutska2022,Taberko2020,
Golovko2018integration} and reviews current challenges and tools for developing
and applying industrial standards alongside modern techniques such as
neuro-control, \emph{neuro-symbolic AI}, and semantic technologies. The main
focus is on standards-related problems and their relevance to today's control
systems. A symbolic neurocontroller is presented as a promising approach for
controlling complex industrial processes such as pasteurization by combining the
strengths of symbolic and neural methods.

\section{History and background: from \texorpdfstring{\textbf{SCADA}}{SCADA} to
\texorpdfstring{\textbf{AI-aided control systems}}{AI-aided control systems}}

Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems have become the
backbone of modern industrial automation. From power generation and water
treatment to food processing, SCADA platforms enable centralized monitoring of
geographically distributed processes \cite{boyer2004scada,Mladen2022}. Their
historical progression explains both current capabilities and the growing need
for intelligent control in pasteurization (Fig.~\ref{fig:scada_timeline}).

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/gleb_scada_timeline.png}
  \caption{Evolution of SCADA architectures from mainframe-based monolithic systems (1960s) to modern AI-integrated control platforms (2020s).}
  \label{fig:scada_timeline}
\end{figure}

Before the 1960s, industrial processes relied on manual monitoring. Operators
read gauges and adjusted controls by hand. This approach was labor-intensive,
error-prone, and limited to small-scale operations. The first generation of
SCADA emerged in the 1960s as mainframe-based monolithic systems with
proprietary protocols and relay logic \cite{boyer2004scada}. Centralized
processing, point-to-point serial links, and basic remote terminal units (RTUs)
characterized this era.

In the 1970s and 1980s, distributed SCADA architectures replaced the single
mainframe. Minicomputers connected via local-area networks shared monitoring and
control responsibilities, improving redundancy yet retaining proprietary
protocols \cite{MFGTechHub2024}. The 1990s brought open standards: TCP/IP,
Ethernet, Modbus, DNP3, and OPC separated data acquisition from presentation
through a client-server model \cite{TechTarget2024}. Wide-area networking and
commercial off-the-shelf hardware reduced costs and eased integration.

Since the 2010s, modern SCADA has converged with the Industrial Internet of
Things (IIoT) and cloud computing. Edge devices preprocess data locally, while
analytics platforms run predictive-maintenance models and detect anomalies
\cite{Nechibvute2024}. Nevertheless, legacy proprietary protocols persist,
integration of heterogeneous vendor equipment remains difficult, and the
introduction of machine learning adds opacity to already complex control loops
\cite{Heidari2025}. These challenges motivate the neuro-symbolic approach
explored in this paper.

Several trends now shape the future of SCADA. Edge computing reduces latency by
processing data near the source. Fifth-generation cellular networks enable
real-time wireless control. Digital twins provide virtual replicas for
simulation and optimization. Artificial intelligence, particularly
neuro-symbolic methods, offers a path toward autonomous yet interpretable
decision-making in safety-critical applications such as pasteurization
\cite{Heidari2025}. These developments set the stage for the standards,
architectures, and experimental results presented in the following sections.
